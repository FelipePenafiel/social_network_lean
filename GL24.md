# What [GL24] supplies

[GL24] is A. Galves and K. Laxa, *Fast consensus and metastability in a highly
polarized social network*, [arXiv:2202.12871v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12871)
(`GL24.pdf` at the root of this repository). It is the `M = 2` case of
arXiv:2607.19651, and several proofs of arXiv:2607.19651 are shortened because they
follow it closely. This file records, statement by statement, what [GL24] writes out
that the paper leaves implicit, and how the formalisation uses it. Page numbers are
those of `GL24.pdf`.

## 2. Steps the paper shortens and [GL24] writes out

### 2.1 The greedy run does not revisit `u` — [GL24] Corollary 8, p. 10

The proof of Proposition 9 needs a greedy run that reaches `L` "without visiting `u`",
and reads it off Proposition 7, which says only where the run ends. [GL24] states the
clause and proves it:

> **Corollary 8.** For any `u ∉ L` and for any `m ≥ 1`,
> `P(Ũ_m^{β,u} = u | ⋂_{j≤m} M_j^u) = 0`.
>
> *Proof.* It follows directly by Part 2 of Proposition 5, by contradiction.

Written out: suppose the greedy run returns to `u` at some `m ≤ (M+1)N`. The
realisation that repeats its first `m` jumps for ever is again greedy at every step,
since the greedy event at a step depends only on the state and the jump at that step,
and it is at `u` at every multiple of `m`. Proposition 7 puts it in `L` at step
`(M+1)N`, and `L̂` is stable under a greedy step (Remark 5), so it is in `L̂` at every
later step, including a multiple of `m`. Hence `u ∈ L̂`, against the hypothesis.

In Lean the argument is deterministic: `skeleton_ne_of_greedy` is stated on a
realisation, the periodic realisation is `fun n => ω (n % m)`, and
`Trajectory.state_add` is the periodicity.

### 2.2 Corollary 10 — [GL24] Corollary 12, pp. 17–18

[GL24] follows the same route as the paper: the `2N` predecessors of the zero list
are `σ(v)` and `-σ(v)` for `v = (0, 1, …, 1)`; invariance turns `μ̃(0)` into
`2N P(Ũ_1^{β,v} = 0) μ̃(v)`; and the step into `0` costs
`1/(2 + (N-1)(e^β + e^{-β})) ≤ e^{-β}`. In Lean these are `zeroPredecessor`,
`measure_zero_le` and `skeletonKernel_zeroPredecessor_le`. [GL24] asserts the
description of the predecessors; here it is proved
(`eq_zeroPredecessor_of_express_eq_zero`), together with the null row that makes it
exhaustive (`measure_exists_zero_row_eq_one`).

### 2.3 The display inside the proof of Theorem 2, part 2 — [GL24] p. 19

Lemma 13 needs a bound on `P(R^{β,u}(L) > t)` that the paper displays inside its
proof of part 2 of Theorem 2 but never states. [GL24] writes that proof out, as its
equations (16)–(18):

* split on the greedy event; on it, Proposition 5.2 puts the process in `L` by step
  `3(N-1)`, so `R(L) ≤ T_{3(N-1)}`;
* the complement costs `1 - ζ_β^{3(N-1)}`, which is Remark 4 here;
* the exit rate of any non-null list exceeds `e^β`, and the greedy event keeps the
  process off `0`, so `T_{3(N-1)}` is dominated by a sum of `3(N-1)` i.i.d.
  exponentials of mean `e^{-β}`, and a union bound finishes.

The paper's display is the same inequality, with `(M+1)N` for `3(N-1)` and
`e^{β/(M-1)}` for `e^β`. The Lean proof (`probHittingGT_ladderSet_le_of_ne_zero`,
blueprint `aux-hitting-rate`) follows those three steps. Its main cost is the clock:
the law of one holding time given the past, the fact that a greedy run never sits at
the zero matrix, and Proposition 8 transported to the sample space of the
continuous-time process (`aux-holding-times`). Part 2 of Theorem 2 is the limit of
this display at `t = e^{-β(1-δ)/(M-1)}`. Both rest on Proposition 7.

### 2.4 Equation (19) — [GL24] Corollary 13, p. 19

[GL24]'s Corollary 13 is the paper's Corollary 11. Its proof is one line: from the
null list the first jump time is exponential of mean `1/(MN)`, and after it
Theorem 2.2 applies. Equation (19) is that decomposition. It needs only the restart
of the process at its first jump and the almost-sure positivity of the holding times,
because the supremum over `u ≠ 0` is on its own right-hand side, so it is proved
outright (`probHittingGT_ladderSet_zero_le`, blueprint `eq19`).

### 2.5 The minorisation of the skeleton — [GL24] p. 15

[GL24] supplies the minorisation of the skeleton chain:

> for any `u' ∈ S`,  `P(Ũ_{n+2N} = l | Ũ_n = u') ≥ ζ_β^N ε*`,

where `l` is the fixed ladder `l(a) = a - 1` and `ε*` is the minimum, over the finite
set of lists with `max|v(a)| < N`, of the probability of reaching `l` in `N` further
steps. This minorises the `2N`-step kernel by `ε δ_l`, uniformly in the starting
point. Both halves of Theorem 1.2 for the skeleton follow, and both are proved in
`SocialNetwork/Doeblin.lean` for a general Markov kernel on a countable space:

* **uniqueness** (`eq_of_invariant_of_iterate_minorisation`): `μ ↦ μ K^{2N}`
  contracts by `1 - ε`. The Lean proof works with `T = ∑ₓ min(μ x, ν x)` and shows
  `T ≥ T + c(1-T)`, which is the total-variation argument in coordinates;
* **existence** (`exists_invariant_of_iterate_minorisation`): [GL24] goes through
  positive recurrence. On a countable space the excursion measure
  `ν(y) = ∑ₘ P_l(Ũₘ = y, R_l > m)` is a sum in `[0,∞]`, the minorisation makes it
  summable directly, and invariance is the last-exit decomposition.

The minorisation itself is `minorisation_iterateKernel`, in
`SocialNetwork/Minorisation.lean`, following the paper's p. 17, which is [GL24]'s
argument carried over. It differs in two ways. `ε*` is explicit: from a matrix with
entries at most `R` in absolute value, any one expression has probability at least
`e^{-2βR/(M-1)}/MN`, so with `R = MN(M-1) + N(M-1)`,
`ε* ≥ (e^{-2βR/(M-1)}/MN)^N`, and the finite set is never exhibited. And the sweep is
proved to reach `l^o` from any matrix whatever, which is what lets the minorising
measure be a single Dirac mass.

### 2.6 Remark 6 — [GL24] Remark 14, p. 19

[GL24]'s Remark 14 is the `M = 2` case of Remark 6, proved "following the same steps
of the proof of Part 2 of Theorem 2". Those steps have to be run at the horizon `N`
rather than `(M+1)N`. From `C^o` the greedy run reaches a ladder for `o` in `N`
expressions, while continuing to `(M+1)N` leaves `L^o` again. So the route is the
last stage of Proposition 7, consensus to a ladder, and Remark 6 is proved outright.

### 2.7 Non-explosion — [GL24] Theorem 1.1, pp. 12–14

[GL24] proves non-explosion by a graphical construction: a rate-1 Poisson point
process in the plane drives the chain, the slow jumps (made by an actor of pressure
`< N` in absolute value) are a thinning of a rate-`λ` Poisson process, and its
Lemma 10 bounds the fast jumps between two slow ones by `N`. The paper asserts the
resulting sandwich (11) without constructing it. The construction is formalised in
`SocialNetwork/Band.lean`, `SocialNetwork/BandCollapse.lean` and
`SocialNetwork/Graphical.lean`, with two substitutions, neither of which changes a
step:

* Mathlib has no Poisson point process, so the marks are carried by the jump-hold
  form. `SocialNetwork.markLaw` draws a mark of the band of [GL24]'s Figure 2 and
  then holds for an exponential time of the band's height `Λ = λ + q^>(v)`. That is
  the same law, and the strip `[0, λ)` still has mass `λ/Λ` whatever the matrix.
* "`T^λ` is a rate-`λ` Poisson process, so `sup T^λ_n = ∞`" becomes
  `SocialNetwork.measure_holdBlowUp_eq_one`, a supermartingale criterion, from
  [GL24]'s own two estimates: `q^< ≤ λ` (`SocialNetwork.lowRate_le_clockBound`) and
  Lemma 10, read on the marks (`SocialNetwork.exists_isLambdaAt_block`).

Neither source proves that the constructed process is the one (3) defines; [GL24]
uses it in passing. That identification is
`SocialNetwork.map_markPathMeasure_collapse_eq`: the accepted marks carry the law of
one step (`SocialNetwork.stepLaw`), shown through a one-mark recursion whose exact
fixed point is `(∑_{p ∈ B} r_p / q)(1 - e^{-qt})`.

The band is built over an arbitrary state space, so Theorem 16, the biased twin,
instantiates the same theorem with the rates of (7) and Proposition 21 in place of
Proposition 5, which is what Appendix C prescribes.

### 2.8 Lemma 14 — [GL24] Proposition 9, pp. 10–11

The paper says its Lemma 14 follows [GL24]'s Proposition 8; in v4 that is
Proposition 9, pp. 10–11. There the two ways to leave `C^+` are an actor of positive
pressure expressing against it (rate `≤ (N-1)e^{-β}`) and two such expressions by
null-pressure actors in a row (a geometric count with parameter
`2(N-1)/((N-1) + e^β)`), each dominated by an exponential. Lemmas 14 and 29 are
proved from the same rates, composed by a first-step comparison with an exponential
clock ([`FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md`](FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md) §2.10).

## 3. Lemmas 19 and 20 — [GL24] Lemmas 6 and 7, pp. 8–9

Both proofs are complete in [GL24], and neither transposes as it stands, for the same
reason: `M = 2` makes the pressures integers, while the paper's Definition 5 carries a
fractional offset `r ∈ [0, 1)` that has no counterpart in [GL24].

### 3.1 Lemma 19 — [GL24] Lemma 6, p. 8

At time `τ(u) - 1` the actors that have expressed carry the partial sums of the
opinions expressed after them, so consecutive ones differ by exactly `1` ([GL24]'s
equation (4)), and the actor of null initial pressure that has not expressed differs
from the first of them by `1` as well (its equation (5)). The values form a `±1` path,
which passes through every integer between `0` and its maximum `m`: that gives the
`m + 1` actors, and [GL24]'s Figure 1 is the picture of it.

The paper differs in two ways:

* its `τ(u)` stops only at the first repeat, while [GL24]'s also stops at the first
  expression by an actor of null initial pressure. That clause is what puts the
  unexpressed null-pressure actor at the end of the path, and what makes `τ(u) ≤ N`
  rather than `N + 1`;
* with `M ≥ 3` the path along a fixed column has steps in `{+1, -1/(M-1)}` and does
  not hit every integer.

What carries over is a first-passage argument: the upward steps have size `1`, so the
first actor whose column-`o` value reaches `j + r` is below `j + 1 + r`, and the first
passages of `r, 1 + r, …, ⌊m⌋ + r` are distinct actors. That is the construction the
paper's "by (25)" points to, and Lemma 19 is proved with it, along the paper's own
case split ([`FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md`](FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md) §2.12).

### 3.2 Lemma 20 — [GL24] Lemma 7, p. 9

[GL24]'s induction is the paper's with the indices shifted by one. It tracks witnesses
at levels `0, 1, …, n(u) - 1`, level `0` being the actor of null pressure, and the
actor reset at each step is the argmax, whose value is `≥ n(u)`, so it is never one
of them. The invariant

> `{1, …, n(u)} ⊆ {Ũ_k(a)}` and `Ũ_k(a) ≥ -(n(u) - (k+1))` for all `a`

is preserved for `k = 1, …, n(u) - 1`, and at `k = n(u) - 1` the lower bound is `0`,
which is `C^+`.

The paper tracks levels `1, …, n(u)` instead, and the argmax is the top witness, so
the level lost to the reset has to be replaced from below. With `r = 0` this is free:
a freshly reset actor is at `0` and gains `+1`, which is level `1`. With `r > 0` it is
not, since level `1` demands `≥ 1 + r`. And for `M ≥ 4` the statement itself fails:
Definition 5 bounds the other columns by `n(u) + r - 1/(M-1)`, and once the witnesses
are reset nothing keeps column `o` above that bound
([`FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md`](FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md) §1.1 has the counterexample). [GL24]
never meets this, since `M = 2` forces `r = 0`. What the paper's Lemma 20 appears to
need is a hypothesis carrying the staircase that Lemma 19's proof builds on column
`o`, which Definition 5 forgets.

## 4. Proposition 12 remains a citation

[GL24] proves its own metastability without [LM22], from Lemma 16 (p. 20, a
non-asymptotic bound `P(U_s^{β,l} ∉ L) ≤ (1 - μ_β(L))/μ_β(L)` from invariance and
symmetry), asymptotic memorylessness and a dyadic iteration. What it obtains is the
qualitative limit. The paper's Proposition 12, and `metastability` here, carry a rate,
`K' β³ e^{-min(δ/3,1/2,θ)β}`, which [GL24] does not give. So the axiom is not
discharged by [GL24], though the qualitative form has a self-contained proof in its §7.

## 5. What [GL24] does not reach

The biased model. [GL24]'s robot (§3 and §8) adds an actor that is not influenced and
always expresses `+1` at rate `e^{βα}`; the paper's bias `α` instead reweights every
actor's rates through the memory profile of equation (6). These are different
perturbations, and [GL24]'s `M̂_n^u` splits on `max|u(a)|` against `α` rather than on
a near-greedy event. So:

* **Proposition 22**, the near-greedy transport of Proposition 6, has no counterpart
  in [GL24]; it is proved along the paper's argument;
* **Proposition 23** would need biased analogues of Lemmas 19 and 20, which [GL24]
  disposes of with "arguments similar to those used to prove Proposition 5" (p. 25)
  and does not write;
* **Proposition 26** and **Lemma 28** have no [GL24] model: [GL24] proves no
  metastability for the process with a robot.
