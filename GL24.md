polarized social network*, [arXiv:2202.12871v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12871)
(`GL24.pdf` at the root of this repository). It is the `M = 2` case of
arXiv:2607.19651, and several proofs of arXiv:2607.19651 are shortened because
they follow it closely.

That makes it the place to look for the steps the paper leaves implicit. This
file records what was found there, obstruction by obstruction. Page numbers are
those of `GL24.pdf`.

**Reading [GL24] changed no classification in [`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md) by
itself.** An obstruction leaves that file when the statement is *proved*, not
when a proof is located. What this file does is say which of them now have a
written argument to follow, and which do not. Two items of §6 have since been
acted on, and those did change it: §2.1 is proved, §2.6 is split, and part 2

## 2. What [GL24] settles

### 2.1 The greedy run does not revisit `u` — [GL24] Corollary 8, p. 10

This is the one step the proof of Proposition 9 asserts and reads off
Proposition 7, and which [`FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md`](FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md) §1.6 asks the
authors for. **[GL24] states it and proves it.**

> **Corollary 8.** For any `u ∉ L` and for any `m ≥ 1`,
> `P(Ũ_m^{β,u} = u | ⋂_{j≤m} M_j^u) = 0`.
>
> *Proof.* It follows directly by Part 2 of Proposition 5, by contradiction.

Written out, the contradiction is the one the transient needs. Suppose the greedy
run returns to `u` at some `m ≤ (M+1)N`. The realisation obtained by repeating
its first `m` jumps for ever is again greedy at every step — the state sequence is
periodic with period `m`, and the greedy event at a step depends only on the state
and the jump at that step — and it is at `u` at every multiple of `m`. But
Proposition 7 puts it in `L` at step `(M+1)N`, and `L̂` is stable under a greedy
step (Remark 5), so it is in `L̂` at every step after that, including a multiple of
`m`. Hence `u ∈ L̂`, against the hypothesis.

In this repository the argument is *deterministic*: `skeleton_ne_of_greedy` is
stated on a realisation, the periodic realisation is `fun n => ω (n % m)`, and
`Trajectory.state_add` is the periodicity. No probability and no Markov property
are needed, and nothing of the paper's fractional machinery (§3 below) is touched.

**This one is no longer blocked on the paper.** It rests on Proposition 7, exactly
as the paper's proof of Proposition 9 does, and therefore closes with Lemmas 19
and 20.

### 2.2 Corollary 10 — [GL24] Corollary 12, pp. 17–18

Already proved here, and [GL24] confirms the route step for step: the `2N`
predecessors of the zero list are `σ(v)` and `-σ(v)` for `v = (0, 1, …, 1)`;
invariance turns `μ̃(0)` into `2N P(Ũ_1^{β,v} = 0) μ̃(v)`; the step into `0` costs
`1/(2 + (N-1)(e^β + e^{-β})) ≤ e^{-β}`. That is `zeroPredecessor`,
`measure_zero_le` and `skeletonKernel_zeroPredecessor_le` with `M = 2`.

[GL24] asserts the description of the predecessors; here it is proved
(`eq_zeroPredecessor_of_express_eq_zero`), and the null row that makes it
exhaustive is earned rather than assumed (`measure_exists_zero_row_eq_one`).

### 2.3 The display inside the proof of Theorem 2, part 2 — [GL24] p. 19

[`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md) files `probHittingGT_ladderSet_le_of_ne_zero` under
*cited from outside the paper*, on the ground that Theorem 2.2 is a limit and a
limit has thrown the rate away. The rate is not outside the paper: it is the
paper's own proof of Theorem 2.2, and **[GL24] writes that proof out in full**, as
its equations (16), (17) and (18):

* split on the greedy event; on it, Proposition 5.2 puts the process in `L` by
  step `3(N-1)`, so `R(L) ≤ T_{3(N-1)}`;
* the complement costs `1 - ζ_β^{3(N-1)}`, which is Remark 4 here;
* the exit rate of any non-null list exceeds `e^β`, and the greedy event keeps the
  process off `0`, so `T_{3(N-1)}` is dominated by a sum of `3(N-1)` i.i.d.
  exponentials of mean `e^{-β}`, and a union bound finishes.

The paper's display is the same inequality with `(M+1)N` for `3(N-1)` and
`e^{β/(M-1)}` for `e^β`. So it is an *unstated step of the paper's own proof*, not
a citation, and it needs nothing this repository cannot state.

**Done**, along exactly those three steps. What it cost was the clock: the law of
one holding time conditionally on the past, read off the Ionescu–Tulcea kernels
the way `Greedy.lean` reads off the expressed pair; the fact that a greedy run
never sits at the zero matrix, which is where `S`'s null row earns its keep; and
Proposition 8 transported to the sample space of the continuous-time process,
where the pairs are the first marginal of a step. All of that is sorry-free
(`aux-holding-times`); the display itself rests on Proposition 7, and so on
Lemmas 19 and 20.

### 2.4 Equation (19) — [GL24] Corollary 13, p. 19

Same conclusion. [GL24]'s Corollary 13 is the paper's Corollary 11, and its proof
is one line: from the null list the first jump time is exponential of mean
`1/(MN)`, and after it Theorem 2.2 applies. Equation (19) is that decomposition.
It follows from Theorem 2.2, not from Corollary 11 as the paper attributes it, and
Theorem 2.2 is where the rate still exists.

**Now proved, and outright.** Writing it out showed that it does not need
Theorem 2.2 either: the supremum over `u ≠ 0` is on its own right-hand side, so
what the decomposition consumes is only the restart of the process at its first
jump and the almost-sure positivity of the holding times. Corollary 11 does need
Theorem 2.2, and inherits `sorryAx` from it; equation (19) carries none.

### 2.5 Doeblin, p. 15 — the minorisation is explicit

The keystone of [`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md) is "Doeblin's minorisation criterion",
and what is recorded as missing is the criterion. [GL24] supplies the
*minorisation itself*, for this chain, in half a page:

> for any `u' ∈ S`,  `P(Ũ_{n+2N} = l | Ũ_n = u') ≥ ζ_β^N ε*`,

where `l` is the fixed ladder `l(a) = a - 1` and `ε*` is the minimum, over the
finite set of lists with `max|v(a)| < N`, of the probability of reaching `l` in `N`
further steps. That is a minorisation of the `2N`-step kernel by `ε δ_l` —
uniform in the starting point, and at a single point.

That shape is stronger than Doeblin's hypothesis in general, and it splits the
keystone in two:

* **uniqueness** is then elementary — `μ ↦ μ K^{2N}` contracts the total variation
  distance by `1 - ε`, so two invariant probability measures coincide. No Harris
  theory, no irreducibility, no aperiodicity. **Done**, in
  `SocialNetwork/Doeblin.lean`, for a general Markov kernel on a countable space:
  `eq_of_invariant_of_iterate_minorisation`. The Lean proof does not go through
  total variation, which Mathlib has no usable theory of; it works with
  `T = ∑ₓ min(μ x, ν x)` and shows `T ≥ T + c(1-T)` directly, which is the same
  argument in coordinates and needs no signed measures;
* **existence** was recorded here as still needing Markov-chain theory Mathlib
  does not have. [GL24] gets it from positive recurrence: the minorisation gives
  `P(R̃^{β,l}(l) > t) ≤ P(2N · Geom(ζ_β^N ε*) > t)`, hence `E[R̃^{β,l}(l)] < ∞`,
  and then the classical construction of the invariant measure from one excursion.
  **That was wrong about the cost.** On a *countable* state space the construction
  needs no theory: `ν(y) = ∑ₘ P_l(Ũₘ = y, R_l > m)` is a sum in `[0,∞]`, the
  minorisation makes it summable directly — no positive-recurrence theorem in
  between — and invariance is the last-exit decomposition in coordinates. **Done**,
  `SocialNetwork/Doeblin.lean`: `exists_invariant_of_iterate_minorisation`, and
  `existsUnique_invariant_of_iterate_minorisation` for both halves together.

**The minorisation itself is now done too**, in `SocialNetwork/Minorisation.lean`
(`minorisation_iterateKernel`), following the p. 17 argument of the multi-opinion
paper, which is this one carried over. One difference is worth recording. [GL24]
takes `ε*` to be a *minimum over a finite set* — the lists with `max|v(a)| < N` —
of a probability it does not compute. Lean has the explicit constant instead:
from a matrix with entries at most `R` in absolute value, any one expression has
probability at least `e^{-2βR/(M-1)}/MN`, because the rates of (3) are
exponentials of bounded exponents. Taking `R = MN(M-1) + N(M-1)`, which the box
of Proposition 6 cannot leave over `N` further expressions, gives
`ε* ≥ (e^{-2βR/(M-1)}/MN)^N` outright. Nothing is lost by it and the finite set
never has to be exhibited.

The other difference is that the sweep is proved to reach `l^o` **from any
matrix whatever**, which the paper uses but states only for the matrices it has
in hand. That is what allows the minorising measure to be a single Dirac mass,
so it is the step Doeblin's criterion actually rests on.

So `blueprint/blueprint.md`'s audit item is gone: both halves are supplied here,
and Doeblin's criterion — existence and uniqueness, for any Markov kernel on a
countable space, consuming nothing but `Kernel.Invariant` — is the clearest thing
this repository has to offer Mathlib.

## 3. What [GL24] sharpens but does not settle: Lemmas 19 and 20

Both proofs exist in [GL24], and both are complete there. Neither transposes,
and the reason is one and the same: **`M = 2` makes the pressures integers, and
the paper's Definition 5 carries a fractional offset `r ∈ [0, 1)` that has no
counterpart in [GL24].**

### 3.1 Lemma 19 — [GL24] Lemma 6, p. 8

[GL24] constructs the actors the paper asserts. At time `τ(u) - 1` the actors that
have expressed carry the partial sums of the opinions expressed after them, so
consecutive ones differ by exactly `1` (its equation (4)); the actor of null
initial pressure that has *not* expressed differs from the first of them by `1` as
well (its equation (5)). The values therefore form a `±1` path, and a `±1` path
from `0` to its maximum `m` passes through every integer between: that is the
`m + 1` actors, and [GL24]'s Figure 1 is the picture of it.

Two differences from the paper, both deliberate in [GL24] and both load-bearing:

* **`τ(u)` is not the same time.** [GL24] stops at the first repeat *or the first
  expression by an actor of null initial pressure*; the paper stops only at the
  first repeat. That clause is what puts the unexpressed null-pressure actor at
  the end of the path — [GL24]'s equation (5) — and what makes `τ(u) ≤ N` rather
  than the paper's `τ(u) ≤ N + 1`.
* **The steps are `±1`.** With `M ≥ 3` the difference between consecutive actors
  is `+1` on the expressed column and `-1/(M-1)` on the others, so the path along
  a fixed column has steps in `{+1, -1/(M-1)}` and does *not* hit every integer.
  What survives is a first-passage argument: the upward steps have size exactly
  `1`, so the first actor whose column-`o` value reaches `j + r` has value `≥ j + r`,
  and the first passages of `r, 1+r, …, ⌊m⌋+r` are distinct actors. That is a
  construction, and it is the one the paper needs; it is not [GL24]'s.

### 3.2 Lemma 20 — [GL24] Lemma 7, p. 9

[GL24]'s induction is the paper's with the indices shifted by one, and the shift
is the whole point. [GL24] tracks witnesses at levels `0, 1, …, n(u)-1` — level `0`
being the actor of null pressure that `S` provides — and the actor reset at each
step is the argmax, whose value is `≥ n(u)`, so it is *never* one of them. The
invariant

> `{1, …, n(u)} ⊆ {Ũ_k(a)}` and `Ũ_k(a) ≥ -(n(u) - (k+1))` for all `a`

is then preserved for `k = 1, …, n(u)-1`, and at `k = n(u)-1` the lower bound is
exactly `0`, which is `C^+`.

The paper tracks levels `1, …, n(u)` instead, and the argmax *is* the top witness,
so the level lost to the reset has to be replaced from below. With `r = 0` the
replacement is free — a freshly reset actor is at `0` and gains `+1`, which is
level `1`. **With `r > 0` it is not**: level `1` demands `≥ 1 + r` and the reset
actor reaches only `1`. That is the break
[`FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md`](FOR-THE-AUTHORS.md) §1.2 records, and [GL24] cannot mend
it, because `M = 2` forces `r = 0`.

So the request to the authors stands, and it is sharper than before: **the
invariant of Lemma 20 has to be one that survives a fractional `r`, and the case
`r = 0` — where [GL24]'s does survive — is not the case that needs it.**

## 4. What [GL24] confirms

* **Poisson point processes** (Theorem 1.1, pp. 13–14). [GL24] proves
  non-explosion by a graphical construction: a rate-1 Poisson point process in the
  plane drives the chain, the slow jumps (those made by an actor of pressure
  `< N` in absolute value) are a thinning of a rate-`λ` Poisson process, and its
  Lemma 10 bounds the fast jumps between two slow ones by `N`. The route is the
  one `blueprint/blueprint.md` records as absent from Mathlib, so the diagnosis
  for Theorems 1.1 and 16 is confirmed, not weakened.
* **Appendix B** (Lemma 14, Lemma 29). The paper says its Lemma 14 follows
  [GL24]'s Proposition 8; in v4 that is Proposition 9, pp. 10–11, and it is
  written out: the two ways to leave `C^+` are an actor of positive pressure
  expressing against it (rate `≤ (N-1)e^{-β}`) and two such expressions by
  null-pressure actors in a row (a geometric count with parameter
  `2(N-1)/((N-1) + e^β)`), each dominated by an exponential. That is continuous-time
  analysis — an exponential race, a geometric number of i.i.d. exponentials — so
  the Mathlib obstruction stands, but the argument to formalise is now written
  down rather than described as "technical".
* **Proposition 12 is genuinely a citation, for the statement as formalised.**
  [GL24] proves its own metastability without [LM22], from Lemma 16 (p. 20: a
  non-asymptotic bound `P(U_s^{β,l} ∉ L) ≤ (1 - μ_β(L))/μ_β(L)` out of invariance
  and symmetry), asymptotic memorylessness, and a dyadic iteration. But what it
  obtains is the *qualitative* limit. The paper's Proposition 12, and
  `metastability` here, carry a rate — `K' β³ e^{-min(δ/3,1/2,θ)β}` — and [GL24]
  gives no rate. So the axiom is not discharged by [GL24]; what changes is that
  "nothing in this library can discharge it" should read "nothing can discharge
  the quantitative form, and the qualitative form has a self-contained proof in
  [GL24] §7".

## 5. What [GL24] does not reach

The biased model. [GL24]'s robot (§3 and §8) adds an actor that is not influenced
and always expresses `+1` at rate `e^{βα}`; the paper's bias `α` instead reweights
every actor's rates through the memory profile of equation (6). They are different
perturbations, and [GL24]'s `M̂_n^u` splits on `max|u(a)|` against `α` rather than
on a near-greedy event. So

* **Proposition 22** — the near-greedy transport of Proposition 6 — has no
  counterpart in [GL24], and the objection recorded against it is untouched;
* **Proposition 23** would need biased analogues of Lemmas 19 and 20, which
  [GL24] disposes of with "arguments similar to those used to prove Proposition 5"
  (p. 25) and does not write;
* **Proposition 26** and **Lemma 28** have no [GL24] model at all: [GL24] proves
  no metastability for the process with a robot.
